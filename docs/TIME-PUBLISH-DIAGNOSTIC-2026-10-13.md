## Tracing without changing gameplay or budgets

`TimeService:publish()` at 1 Hz writes deduped attributes to the **authored** `Workspace.Vitriol.World.TimeAnchor` and sends the existing 9-argument `RE_TimeSync` snapshot to clients. It also runs outside the slot when a prep clock charge, fade stage or debug seek needs an immediate snapshot. The new read-only cadence call returned `playing:false`: **no live samples or root-cause attribution were available** after the user's Play had stopped.

A read-only 10,000-iteration **Edit-mode** microbenchmark of `TimeAnchor:GetAttribute` and `TimeModel.cycleSeconds` found ~0.00018 ms/read and ~0.00027 ms/cycle call (`tools/reports/time-publish-read-cost.json`). These numbers do **not** include `SetAttribute`, replication, `FireAllClients`, or Play scheduler contention. They make removing a handful of pure reads an unconvincing remedy for 0.389 ms. No speculative reduction to the 1 Hz snapshot contract or change to clock/Day-prep/fade semantics was made.

Instead, scheduled `Time:Publish` now records low-frequency `os.clock` sections for attribute publication and remote dispatch, with paired components for its largest measured **same-sample** total (`TimeService.publishCost`). Immediate publishes are excluded so the Slow-slot diagnostic remains interpretable. Cadence still times/warns on the entire slot with the **unchanged 0.050 ms share**; `publishCost` does not gate or suppress it. `jobs/probe_creature_cadence_live_vitriol.lua` now exposes `Time:Publish` Cadence avg/peak/sample/skipped and `TimeService.publishCost` alongside the earlier Think/Session rows **while Play is active**. Its lifetime peak is not identical to the Cadence profiler's resettable window peak. The section timing itself adds a few clock reads once per second; it is diagnostic, not a performance fix or budget acceptance.
