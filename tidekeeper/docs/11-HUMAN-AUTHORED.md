# 11 — Human-Authored Work

Everything an AI agent cannot do, or can do but must not be trusted to judge. No code
in this document — these are briefs and acceptance criteria for a person.

---

## 1. Why this document exists

An agent with Studio MCP access can build the Explorer tree, execute Luau, run a
playtest and take a screenshot. It cannot tell you whether the moment the tide turns
feels good. Keeping that boundary explicit is what stops an iteration from claiming a
game is finished when it is merely assembled.

Rule: **any item in this document that an iteration marks "done" must name the human who
did it and what they observed.**

## 2. The island (it4 blockout, it5 finish)

**Brief.** A low, wet, grey-green island with a ruined lighthouse at its centre and
twelve piers radiating outward. The player's post sits at the head of their pier. Beyond
the piers, the flats — a wide, almost-flat plain that the water crosses four times a
real day.

| Requirement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Total walkable radius | ~420 studs |
| Flats gradient | must be shallow enough that a 26.8-stud spring range translates to a *visible* 130+ stud horizontal water movement |
| Inner / Mid / Outer / Bar zone boundaries | at heights 2 / −2 / −6 / −11 (doc 07 §2) — the terrain must actually produce these |
| Parts at rest | ≤ 1 800 (doc 01 §6) |
| Walk time, post → Bar | 130 s ± 15 s. **Measure it.** |
| Landmarks | ≥ 6 distinct silhouettes visible from the flats, so players can navigate without a minimap |

**Acceptance:** stand on the Bar at spring low water and look back at your post. It must
feel far, and the way back must be legible.

**Anti-requirement:** no fog that hides the tide line. The whole game is watching the
water.

## 3. Water

One plane part, driven by `TideController`. This is the most-looked-at object in the game.

- Height set every frame from `Tide.height` — never tweened, never interpolated from a
  replicated value.
- The **edge** matters more than the surface. Where water meets sand needs a wet-sand
  gradient, foam, and a slight lag. Players read the tide from the edge.
- Must look correct at the moment of slack water, when it is barely moving.
- Must be cheap: one part, one shader, no reflections on mobile.

**Acceptance:** watch a full 12 h 25 m cycle at 60× and the water must never pop,
stutter, or reverse.

## 4. Audio

The largest single lever on this game's feel, per doc 07 §7.

| Cue | Brief | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sea bed | Continuous loop, filtered by `Tide.velocity` | Must be tolerable for 40 minutes |
| **Turn of the tide** | A one-shot at each state change | Players must learn to recognise it without being told. Test this. |
| Comb, per material family (8) | Short, tactile, distinct | Rarity must be *audible* before it is visible |
| Ledger footsteps | Paces the replay | A 12-entry Ledger must feel like a visit, not a list |
| Dispatch | The one moment of departure | Should feel slightly sad |
| Music (6 cues) | Tide-state-selected, ≤ 25% of mix, **off by default on mobile** | Must not loop audibly within 3 minutes |

## 5. Meshes and icons

Generated by the pipeline in doc 09 §2, but **curated** here.

| Gate | Criterion | Reject rate to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silhouette | Readable in solid black at 48 px | ~20% |
| Family coherence | Looks like it came from the same world as the other 199 | ~15% |
| Scale | Correct against a reference stud cube | ~10% |
| Pivot | At the base, centred | ~10% |
| Triangles | ≤ 900 | mechanical |
| Atlas | Fits its family's 1024² sheet | mechanical |

**Acceptance:** lay all 200 icons out in one 48 px grid. If any one of them draws the eye
for the wrong reason, or if any two are confusable, fix or cut.

## 7. The Ledger screen — the one that must be prototyped early

**This is R1 in the risk register and the highest-value human task in the project.**

Before iteration 5 builds any content, a human must sit real players in front of a
working Ledger replay and watch their faces. Specifically:

- Do they read the names?
- Do they react to a refusal?
- Do they go and look at the shelf the visitor wanted?
- Do they *care* that it was a real person?

If the answer to the last one is no, the concept needs rework, and that is a
cheap discovery at iteration 4 and a ruinous one at launch.

**Acceptance:** five players, unprompted, in a 10-minute session. Record whether any of
them mentions a visitor by name afterwards.

## 8. Studio-only verification checklist

Things no headless harness can establish. Iteration 4 must do these with MCP + a human
watching, and record the result.

- [ ] `buffer` + base64 round-trip of a real 6 KB post blob through a real DataStore
- [ ] A real `MemoryStore` HashMap `UpdateAsync` under simulated contention
- [ ] Real `Tide.height` per-frame cost on the client MicroProfiler
- [ ] Water plane at 60 fps on a low-end device
- [ ] `BindToClose` completing 12 saves inside 25 s
- [ ] The orthographic Chart camera actually rendering as intended (RDC 2026 feature, beta)
- [ ] UI blur / text shadows present or gracefully absent (doc 08 §5 contingency)
- [ ] Touch targets ≥ 44 px on a real 5-inch phone, in sunlight
- [ ] Notification arriving on a real device at the predicted second
- [ ] Time to first interactive ≤ 6 s on a mid-tier phone over mobile data
