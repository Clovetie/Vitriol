# 00 — Vision

**Codename:** TIDEKEEPER
**Status:** Iteration 1 (research + design). No gameplay code exists yet, by design.
**Relationship to VITRIOL:** none. Separate game, separate tree (`tidekeeper/`), separate
tooling copy (`tidekeeper/tools/`). Nothing in this folder may `require` anything from
`/code` and nothing in `/code` may reference this folder.

---

## 1. The one-line pitch

> A cozy island trading post where the sea rises and falls on a real clock, and every
> stranger who visits while you are offline is a real player.

## 2. The long version

You keep a **Tidepost** on a tidal flat. Twice a real-world day the sea goes out and
uncovers the flats; twice a day it comes back in. When the water is low you walk out and
**beachcomb** — shells, glass, bone, cargo, things that should not be there. When the
water is high, **deep-draught traders** can reach your dock.

You cannot be online all the time, and the game does not want you to be. Before you
leave you set your post's **standing orders** — what it sells, at what price, what it
will accept in barter, who gets served first — and you dispatch your own **Drifter** to
one to three other players' posts.

While you are away, other real players' Drifters arrive at your post and are served by
your standing orders. When you come back, the first thing you do is **read the Ledger**:
who came, what they wanted, what they took, what they left, and what they wrote to you.

That Ledger is the game.

## 3. Design pillars

Every feature must serve one of these. If it serves none, it is cut.

### P1 — Absence is content
Time away from the game is the game's raw material. The reward for leaving is a richer
return. There is no energy meter, no punishment for absence, and no requirement to be
online at a specific minute. A player who plays once a day for six minutes must have a
complete, satisfying experience.

### P2 — Every stranger is a real person, delayed
No filler NPCs in the visitor slot. Every Drifter that docks at your post is a snapshot
of a real account's real preferences, and that account gets a real receipt back. This is
what makes a quiet server feel populated and is the game's single hardest technical
commitment. See [`03-ASYNC-SOCIAL.md`](03-ASYNC-SOCIAL.md).

### P3 — The tide is the clock everyone shares
One deterministic function of wall-clock time drives the whole world. Every server,
every client, and every offline replay computes the same tide with zero replication and
zero storage. It gives the game a shared global schedule — a reason for thousands of
strangers to be doing the same thing at the same odd hour — for free.
See [`02-TIDE-AND-TIME.md`](02-TIDE-AND-TIME.md).

### P4 — Small hands, big consequences
One decision per session should be able to matter. Pricing one shelf wrong should cost
you a week of reputation with one Current. The systems are shallow to operate and deep
to reason about. This is what buys long-session players without excluding the
three-minute player.

### P5 — Determinism or it does not ship
Every simulated outcome is reproducible from `(postState, drifterId, tideEpoch, seed)`.
This is not purity for its own sake: it is what lets the whole simulation be unit-tested
headlessly, verified server-side, replayed for the player as an animated Ledger, and run
identically in an offline solo session. See [`04-SIMULATION-MODEL.md`](04-SIMULATION-MODEL.md).

## 4. Why this game, and why now

The concept is not chosen for novelty. It is chosen because five separate 2026 platform
facts line up behind it, and because it attacks the specific failure mode that kills
almost every new Roblox experience.

### 4.1 The cold-start problem, solved structurally

The standard death of a new Roblox game: a player joins, the server has two other people
in it, nothing is happening, they leave inside thirty seconds. Community analytics put
this at roughly 45% of players lost in the first 30 seconds before the spawn area was
redesigned around a clear first action
([r/robloxgamedev retention teardown](https://www.reddit.com/r/robloxgamedev/comments/1sakqj4/i_tracked_retention_on_my_roblox_game_for_3/)).

TIDEKEEPER's first action on arrival is *reading a full inbox generated by other humans*.
That inbox is non-empty on the very first session (seeded), and it is populated by real
players from the day there are a hundred of them. The game feels busy at 3 CCU. No other
mechanic on the platform does this.

### 4.2 Five platform bets, all announced, all dated

| Platform capability | Status | What TIDEKEEPER does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notification system for **turn-based / asynchronous multiplayer** | Studio Beta Oct 2026, full release by end of 2026 ([RDC 2026](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/09/rdc-2026-the-world-needs-more-play)) | This game *is* the use case. "A deep-water trader is waiting at your dock." |
| **Offline solo play** | early access soon, wide mid-2027 ([RDC 2026](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-unveils-new-play-creation-and-monetisation-tools-at-rdc-2026/)) | Resolve-on-read means the game already works offline by construction. Nearly free. |
| **Browser play in Chrome** | by end of 2026 | Low part count, UI-first, tiny download. Ideal instant-play candidate. |
| **2D / orthographic camera, sprite sheets, UI blur + gradients** | announced RDC 2026 | The Chart, the Ledger and the Almanac become genuinely good-looking screens instead of Roblox-default frames. |
| **Scene Generator, Cube 4D, Procedural Models** | Scene Generator later 2026; 4D open beta Feb 2026 ([Roblox](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/roblox-announces-ai-powered-object-scene-creation-model)) | Generates the 200+ beachcomb finds and post décor set — the one part of this game that is genuinely asset-hungry. |

The bet is that a game designed *for* the async notification system in the quarter it
ships is competing against approximately nobody, while a new cozy simulator is competing
against Grow a Garden.

### 4.3 The genre evidence

The 2026 charts are unambiguous about what retains: cozy daily loops and player-driven
economies. Grow a Garden runs a ~15.7–17.6 minute average session and holds top-of-chart
concurrency on a loop with no combat and no story
([RoMonitor](https://romonitorstats.com/experience/126884695634066/)); Adopt Me has held
half a million concurrents since 2017 almost entirely on its trading economy; Dress to
Impress holds one of the platform's highest approval ratings on nothing but social
judgement of player-made arrangements.

TIDEKEEPER takes the retention shape of the first, the player-economy engine of the
second, and the social-judgement layer of the third, and solves all three of their
weaknesses at once — idle games are lonely, trading economies need liquidity, and
judgement games need people online simultaneously. Asynchronous presence fixes each.

## 5. The core loop

A session is 3–12 minutes and has six beats. A player may stop after beat 2 and still
have had a complete session.

| # | Beat | Time | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Arrive** — tide is wherever the clock says; post is as you left it plus consequences | 0:00–0:15 | Orientation, and the first frame must already contain a number that changed |
| 2 | **The Ledger** — animated replay of who came while you were gone | 0:15–2:00 | The payoff. Named real players, their letters, their receipts |
| 3 | **Beachcomb** (low tide only) or **Dockwork** (high tide only) | 2:00–6:00 | The active verb. Tide decides which one you get — you do not choose |
| 4 | **Stock & price** — arrange shelves, set standing orders | 6:00–9:00 | The decision layer. P4 lives here |
| 5 | **Dispatch** — send your Drifter to 1–3 posts, write a letter | 9:00–11:00 | Your outgoing async turn |
| 6 | **Leave** | — | A notification is scheduled for the next notable event |

## 6. What the player is actually optimising

Three visible numbers, in tension:

- **Stock** — what is on your shelves. Spend it to serve visitors, or hoard it for the Almanac.
- **Standing** — per-Current reputation (five trade routes). Gates *which* Drifters can reach you. Slow to build, fast to lose.
- **Renown** — social score from other players rating your post's arrangement. Gates cosmetics and the Showcase board.

You cannot maximise all three. Serving everyone burns stock. Refusing low-value visitors
protects stock but drops Standing. A beautiful post raises Renown but the good-looking
arrangement is rarely the efficient one.

## 7. Progression

| Tier | Name | Unlock condition | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Driftwood Shack | start | 4 shelf slots, 1 Current, 3 Drifters/day inbound |
| 2 | Stone Pier | 2 000 Standing total | 8 slots, 2 Currents, dock reaches high-tide traders |
| 3 | Lantern Hall | 12 000 Standing + Almanac 40 | 16 slots, 3 Currents, standing orders get conditionals |
| 4 | Harbour Light | 50 000 Standing + Almanac 110 | 24 slots, 5 Currents, you can host other players' posts as tenants |
| P | **Relight the Lighthouse** | tier 4 + a full Current set | Prestige: reset to tier 1 holding a Beacon; Beacons are permanent and visible to every visitor |

Prestige is deliberately late and deliberately social — a Beacon is a thing other players
see when their Drifter docks at your post.

## 8. Scope — what iteration 5 must be able to ship

**In scope for v1.0 (the "amazing" bar):**

- One island archipelago, five Currents, one post per player.
- Full tide simulation, beachcombing, dockwork.
- The Drifter Exchange, end to end, with real cross-player receipts.
- The Ledger as an animated replay, not a text list.
- 200 Almanac finds, 4 post tiers, prestige.
- Standing orders with conditionals.
- Showcase ratings and a weekly Renown board.
- Experience Notifications wired to the async loop.
- Mobile-first UI; must be fully playable one-handed in portrait.

**Explicitly out of scope for v1.0:**

- Combat of any kind.
- Robux-for-item player trading (prohibited — see [`13-RISKS-AND-COMPLIANCE.md`](13-RISKS-AND-COMPLIANCE.md)).
- Paid random items / loot boxes of any kind (deliberate regulatory avoidance).
- Real-time co-presence mechanics beyond "you can see other players walking the flats".
- Voice, user-authored free text beyond a **fixed-phrase letter composer** (safety).
- Cross-server synchronous events.

## 9. Idea bank (not committed, for iterations 5+)

Held here so later iterations do not relitigate them.

- **Storms.** A global weather layer on a 3–5 day cycle that suppresses the tide's
  predictability and floods the flats early. Good; adds variance to a very regular clock.
- **Salvage wrecks.** A shared, server-discovered wreck that all posts in a Current can
  strip for 48 h. The one synchronous event the design allows.
- **Apprentices.** A second, smaller post you configure and cannot visit, run entirely by
  standing orders. Tests whether the standing-order language is expressive enough.
- **The Tide Chart as a puzzle.** An orthographic 2D minigame for predicting where cargo
  will wash up. Directly uses the new 2D engine support.
- **Letters as a discovery surface.** Opt-in, fixed-phrase-only letters that can be
  forwarded. High social value, high moderation cost. Needs policy review.
- **Named Drifters.** After a player's Drifter visits you three times, they get a name in
  your Ledger and a small permanent entry in your Almanac.

## 10. Titles

The codename `TIDEKEEPER` is internal and stable — folder names, module prefixes and
docs use it. The **shipping title** is a discovery decision and should be taken in
iteration 5 against live search data, not now. Roblox discovery rewards literal
verb-a-noun titles ("Grow a Garden", "Steal a Brainrot", "Build a Boat").

Candidates, in current preference order:

1. **Keep a Tidepost** — matches the platform's title grammar, describes the verb.
2. **Mind the Tide** — better line, worse search.
3. **Run a Tide Shop** — most literal, least character.

Decision owner: iteration 5. Recorded in [`../ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md) as an open decision.

## 11. What AI builds vs. what a human must build

See [`09-AI-BUILD-PIPELINE.md`](09-AI-BUILD-PIPELINE.md) for the full breakdown and the
2026 tool survey. The short version:

| Built by an AI agent, headlessly, in iterations 2–4 | Must be done by a human in Studio |
| --- | --- |
| Tide math, RNG, Drifter codec, simulation kernel | Placing the island, feel of the flats, camera |
| Persistence schema, budget accounting, migrations | Verifying real DataStore/MemoryStore behaviour under load |
| Standing-order interpreter and its test suite | Whether the Ledger replay is actually *moving* |
| Economy tables, drop curves, pacing maths | Audio, and the moment the tide turns |
| All UI logic and layout specification | Whether it reads on a 5-inch phone in sunlight |
| Notification scheduling logic | Opt-in copy, and whether the notification is welcome |
